# Red-Flag Checklist

Use this file to decide which clauses to flag and how serious each one is.
Read every clause of the document against the ten general categories, then
run the extra checks for the document type.

## Risk levels

| Level | Use when |
|---|---|
| **High** | The clause could cost the user a lot of money, or removes a key right (to sue, to cancel, to get a refund, to own their work). |
| **Medium** | The clause is unfair, one-sided, or unclear, but the harm is limited or uncertain. |
| **Low** | Worth knowing, but common in this kind of document and rarely a problem. |

### Moving a flag up or down

Start at the default level for the category, then adjust:

- **Raise one level** if the clause has no limit (no cap, no end date, no
  notice), stacks with another flagged clause (for example auto-renewal plus
  a long cancellation notice plus a cancellation fee), or the amount is large
  compared with the deal.
- **Lower one level** if the clause has a fair limit (a cap, a notice period,
  a right for both sides), is required or standard for the document type, or
  only applies in rare situations.
- Never go above High or below Low. If a clause would drop below Low, do not
  flag it.

## Rules for every flag

1. **Quote exactly.** Copy the words from the page, including odd spelling.
   Use "..." only to cut words from the middle of a long quote. No quote
   means no flag.
2. **Explain plainly.** One or two short sentences. Say what could happen to
   the user, in everyday words. Example: "If you pay late, the landlord can
   charge any amount they choose."
3. **Give a question to ask.** A question the user can put to the other side
   or to a lawyer before signing.
4. **Do not flag normal things.** A fair document should produce few flags.
   Signatures, a governing-law line, a standard notice address, or a
   reasonable late fee with a stated cap are not flags on their own.

## 1. Hidden or unclear fees

- **What it is:** Charges that are not stated as a fixed amount, or that the
  other side can set later.
- **Example wording:** "additional charges may apply"; "administrative fee as
  determined by Company"; a late fee with no cap; "plus applicable fees".
- **Why it is risky:** The user cannot know the real cost before signing.
- **Default level:** Medium. High if there is no cap and the fee repeats
  (daily or monthly late fees).
- **Question to ask:** "What exactly are these charges, and what is the most
  I could be charged?"

## 2. Auto-renewal and hard-to-cancel terms

- **What it is:** The agreement renews on its own, or cancelling needs long
  notice, a special method, or a fee.
- **Example wording:** "renews automatically for successive terms"; "written
  notice no less than 90 days before"; "cancellation fee equal to the
  remaining balance".
- **Why it is risky:** The user can get locked into another full term or pay
  for months they do not want.
- **Default level:** Medium. High if notice is 60 days or more, or a
  cancellation fee applies, or both.
- **Question to ask:** "How and by when do I cancel, and is there any fee?"

## 3. One-sided changes

- **What it is:** The other side can change the terms, price, or service
  without the user's agreement.
- **Example wording:** "we may change these terms at any time"; "rates are
  subject to change"; "continued use constitutes acceptance".
- **Why it is risky:** The deal the user signs may not be the deal they get.
- **Default level:** Medium. High if price can change with no notice and no
  right to cancel.
- **Question to ask:** "Will I be told before changes, and can I cancel
  without a fee if I do not agree?"

## 4. Waiver of rights

- **What it is:** The user gives up the right to go to court, join a class
  action, or have a jury.
- **Example wording:** "binding arbitration"; "waives any right to a jury
  trial"; "no class or representative actions"; "exclusive venue in
  [far-away place]".
- **Why it is risky:** Disputes become harder and more costly for the user,
  and small claims may not be worth pursuing at all.
- **Default level:** High. Medium if there is an easy opt-out window (for
  example 30 days by email) or a small-claims court exception.
- **Question to ask:** "Can I opt out of arbitration, and who pays the
  arbitration costs?"

## 5. Unlimited liability or large penalties

- **What it is:** The user is responsible for all losses, or must pay large
  fixed penalties, often while the other side limits its own liability.
- **Example wording:** "you are fully liable for all losses"; "indemnify and
  hold harmless ... including attorneys' fees"; "liquidated damages of";
  "Company's liability shall not exceed $100".
- **Why it is risky:** One problem could cost far more than the whole deal.
- **Default level:** High. Medium if there is a reasonable cap that applies
  to both sides.
- **Question to ask:** "Is there a limit on what I could owe, and does the
  same limit apply to you?"

## 6. Broad data use

- **What it is:** Personal data can be shared, sold, or used for purposes
  beyond the service.
- **Example wording:** "share with our partners and affiliates"; "third
  parties for marketing purposes"; "we may sell or transfer your information".
- **Why it is risky:** The user loses control of who sees their information.
- **Default level:** Medium. High if the document allows selling data or
  sharing sensitive data (health, financial, location, ID numbers).
- **Question to ask:** "Who exactly gets my data, and can I say no?"

## 7. Rights to your content or work

- **What it is:** The other side gets broad or permanent rights to things the
  user creates, uploads, or invents.
- **Example wording:** "perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free
  license"; "all inventions ... whether or not during working hours"; "you
  waive all moral rights".
- **Why it is risky:** The user may lose ownership or control of their own
  work, including work done on their own time.
- **Default level:** Medium. High if it covers work outside the job or
  service, or cannot be ended.
- **Question to ask:** "Does this cover things I make on my own time, and
  can the license end?"

## 8. Vague words that give one side control

- **What it is:** Words that let the other side decide what a term means.
- **Example wording:** "reasonable"; "at our sole discretion"; "from time to
  time"; "as deemed necessary"; "including but not limited to".
- **Why it is risky:** The user cannot predict how the term will be applied.
- **Default level:** Low. Medium if the vague word controls money, ending
  the agreement, or the user's rights. Flag the clause, not every use of the
  word.
- **Question to ask:** "Can we replace this with a specific amount, time, or
  rule?"

## 9. Missing items

- **What it is:** Something important that the document should say but does
  not.
- **Examples:** no refund terms; no end date or term length; no way to cancel;
  no contact for disputes; no total price; blank spaces left unfilled.
- **Why it is risky:** Gaps tend to be settled in favor of the side with more
  power.
- **Default level:** Medium. High for blank spaces in a document about to be
  signed, or a missing total price.
- **How to quote:** There is no text to quote. Instead, quote the nearest
  related clause, or write *Not found in the document* and name the pages
  checked. This is the only kind of flag allowed without a direct quote.
- **Question to ask:** "Can we add [the missing item] in writing before I
  sign?"

## 10. Pressure signs

- **What it is:** Wording that rushes the user or discourages questions.
- **Example wording:** "offer valid today only"; "must be signed within 24
  hours"; "you acknowledge you have had the opportunity to consult a lawyer";
  "this agreement is non-negotiable".
- **Why it is risky:** Pressure keeps people from reading carefully or
  getting help.
- **Default level:** Low. Medium if a short deadline is combined with High
  flags elsewhere.
- **Question to ask:** "Can I have more time to review this?"

## Extra checks by document type

Run these in addition to the ten categories. If the document type is not
listed, use only the ten categories.

### Lease (rental)

- **Security deposit:** amount, when it is returned, what can be deducted,
  any "non-refundable" part. High if all or most is non-refundable.
- **Repairs:** who pays for what. Medium if the tenant must pay for major
  repairs (heating, plumbing, structure).
- **Entry rights:** notice before the landlord enters. Medium if there is
  no notice requirement.
- **Early exit:** cost to leave early. High if the tenant owes all remaining
  rent with no duty for the landlord to re-rent.
- **Rent increases** during the term, guest limits, and automatic renewal.

### Loan or credit

- **Real cost:** APR versus the headline rate; total amount repaid. Flag if
  the APR is missing (Missing items, High).
- **Prepayment penalty:** fee for paying early. Medium; High if large.
- **Collateral:** what can be taken on default (car, home, wages, bank
  account). High for a home or wage assignment.
- **Default terms:** what counts as default (for example one late payment),
  and whether the whole balance becomes due at once ("acceleration"). High.
- **Variable rate:** how high it can go. High if there is no cap.
- **Co-signer duties**, balloon payments, and add-on insurance.

### Employment

- **Non-compete:** length, distance, and job scope. High if over 1 year or
  very broad. Note that some places do not enforce non-competes.
- **Pay and overtime:** salary, overtime rules, unpaid trial periods.
  High if overtime is excluded without explanation.
- **Ownership of work:** see category 7. High if it covers personal projects.
- **Termination:** notice, severance, and "for cause" definitions.
- **Clawbacks:** repaying training costs, bonuses, or relocation if leaving.
  Medium; High if large or long.

### Terms of service (apps, websites, subscriptions)

- **Account removal:** can they close the account without notice or refund?
  Medium.
- **Data sale and sharing:** see category 6.
- **Changes without notice:** see category 3.
- **Free trial to paid:** does a trial turn into a paid plan on its own?
  Medium; High if cancellation is hard.
- **Content license:** see category 7.

### Insurance

- **Exclusions:** what is not covered. High for common events in the user's
  situation (for example flood in a flood area, pre-existing conditions).
- **Limits and deductibles:** per claim and per year. Medium if low or
  unclear.
- **Claim deadlines:** time to report a claim. High if very short (for
  example 72 hours).
- **Cancellation by insurer**, premium increases, and waiting periods.

## When the document looks fair

If no clause reaches Low or above, say "No red flags were found in the
pages read." Never say the document is safe, fair, or fine to sign. Still
include the "Before you sign" checklist and the disclaimer.
